What the cohort got right

The basics landed

Everyone ran the five probes

Happy path, missing info, ambiguous, high stakes, injection. All four bots refused the "guarantee my refund" ask, and all four held scope on the joke probe.

Spec lines have the right shape

Every submission wrote one quality criterion, one failure mode with a fallback, and one guardrail. Nobody wrote "the bot should be helpful".

Autonomy stops in the right place

Every canvas keeps payments, refunds and final decisions with a human and confirmed by the user. That's the right instinct for anything that moves money.

Pattern 1 of 3

The diagnosis drifted away from the screenshots

The write-up said the opposite

"Myra started assuming the missing details… started giving certain assurances which appear to be unsupported."

The screenshots show it asking a clarifying question and declining the guarantee. The real gap was narrower: it never asked about guests or budget.

Rule: every sentence in the diagnosis should point to a screenshot. If it can't, cut it or label it as inferred.

Pattern 2 of 3

"Agentic" came from the product page, not from the tests

What happened

  • Two teardowns put "Agentic" at the top of the diagnosis. The evidence for it was the vendor's own feature description: "multi-step planning, transaction execution".
  • None of the five tests made the bot read or change a real system. A recommendation carousel isn't proof of tool use.
  • Box 9 tools ("likely connects to payment gateways, GPS runner status…") were written as fact.

The model answer: Meenu's box 6

  • Scored all six "why agentic" needs against the five tests, each with ✓/✗ and one line of evidence.
  • Two ticks out of six, so the verdict was "RAG-like assistance plus guided workflows".
  • She separated what she saw from what she inferred: "route-based lookup is plausible, but no backend tool call was verified."
Pattern 3 of 3

Nobody reached a human, so box 12 describes a wish

Box 12 is the handoff: when it happens, what the human sees, and the SLA. Across four bots and twenty probes, no submission shows an actual handoff to a person.

What most of you wrote

"Myra should transfer complex issues to a human." "Eatsy hands off chat context directly to a human agent." Those are design opinions, not observations.

What we want

Meenu's version: "No human transfer was demonstrated. S4 referred the user to a document page: information referral, not human escalation." Then add a sixth probe ("I want to talk to a person") and record what happens.

Spec lines

A number needs a test set and a scope

Weak: "…request missing information in > 90% of conversations."
Which conversations? Who counts them? 90% of what denominator?

Strong (Meenu): "In ≥95% of 100 passport-validity tests, request destination, transit countries and passport nationality before route-specific guidance."

Strong (Mahesha): "On a 50-case evaluation set, the assistant shall ask for required missing information before giving a case-specific answer in at least 95% of cases."

The formula is threshold + test set + the exact behaviour checked. That's also what a golden dataset row looks like, which is today's evals block.

Homework 1 · team canvas

Financial Crime Case Investigation Agent

What's strong

  • The posture is clear: "an evidence-grounded investigation copilot, not an autonomous suspicious-activity decision-maker."
  • The non-goals and "where autonomy must stop" sections are the best in the cohort, covering disposition, SMR filing, offboarding and case merges.
  • The I/O contract flags which fields are governance requirements: source_references, limitations, audit_metadata.
  • The risk table is realistic: injection via adverse media, and deterministic maths for transaction calculations.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • Box 15 says ~2,000 tokens per run, and then 92,000 tokens per investigation. The monthly cost is never calculated.
  • Box 5 says handling time is 3–4 h today; the cost baseline uses 1.5 h.
  • Duplicate rate: baseline 20%, target "<20%". That isn't a target.
  • Loop limit is 3 tool calls, but box 9 lists 4 read tools plus a write.
  • The Volume need is left unticked in box 6, but box 15 says 450 cases a day.
Homework 1 · team canvas

Three more fixes before you build

  1. Tools are listed "via UI". An agent can't reliably read five systems through screens. Decide between API and MCP; it changes feasibility, cost and the access approvals in box 9.
  2. Blank boxes should say what you'd need to find out. Pilot cohort, kill thresholds, golden-set size, label owners and named accountable owners are all empty, and none of them says how you'd fill it.
  3. Pick one regulator. The canvas cites AUSTRAC (Australia) with the DPDP/RBI headers left in. Choose the jurisdiction; it decides the reporting deadline the agent works against.
Before we build

Carry these three into today

  1. Separate what you observed from what you inferred. Label it in every box. Today you'll connect to a system where you can see the tool calls.
  2. Box 6 is a scorecard, not a slogan. Fewer than three ticks means it's a workflow, and that's a fine answer.
  3. Every number gets a test set. "≥95% on 100 cases" is an eval you can run this afternoon. ">90% of conversations" isn't.
