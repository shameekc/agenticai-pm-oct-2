Agentic AI for Product ManagersOct 24

Shift 01 · Junior PM hiring is getting squeezed

~70%

of senior product VPs in one recent roundtable said they’ve stopped hiring associate or junior PMs.

The expectation instead: existing PMs build agents and workflows that absorb that workload.

Anecdotal: a conversation with 10+ Fortune 500 product leaders, not a survey.

Shift 01 · continued

The work that used to train junior PMs is going to agents.

Customer-feedback analysis

Competitive research

Reporting

Customer calls into insights

Shift 02 · The handoff is changing

Product and engineering are blurring.

PMs now

  • Prototype and connect APIs
  • Design agent workflows
  • Test model outputs
  • Bring a working prototype to the conversation

Engineers now

  • Get closer to customers
  • Question requirements
  • Spot customer problems
  • Help decide what gets built

Both need to understand the customer and the system.

Shift 03 · Leaders are bullish on PMs who can build

“There’s always room for an AI-pilled PM with real technical intuition.”

A senior product leader, on who they’re still hiring

Shift 03 · What technical intuition looks like

Five questions you should be able to answer for any customer problem

01Does this need an agent, a simpler workflow, or no AI at all?
02What context and tools does the model need?
03How will we evaluate whether it works?
04Where should a human approve the action?
05What happens to cost, latency and reliability as usage grows?

Shift 03 · The new credibility bar

“I know how to use ChatGPT” no longer counts.

1

Build it

Something real, around a real customer problem.

2

Explain the trade-offs

Why this design, what it costs, where it breaks.

3

Make it reliable

Working for customers, not just in a demo.

Shift 04 · Leaders are back in the details

Senior leaders feel the AI skills gap themselves.

How do you…

Evaluate an AI roadmap if you don’t know what’s feasible?

How do you…

Challenge a cost estimate or assess a demo?

How do you…

Know your team is making the right trade-offs?

You can delegate implementation. You can’t delegate understanding.
